Behavioral Geometric Supervision Aligns Video Foundation Models with Human Social Perception

Kathy Garcia Leyla Isik
Johns Hopkins University
Paper arXiv VSS 2026 poster Code (coming soon) BibTeX

Today's best video models — including V-JEPA 2 — organize social scenes less like humans than a sentence embedding of a caption does. A compact behavioral signal fixes that: fine-tuning on human similarity geometry nearly triples alignment, beats language baselines, and transfers to abstract social interactions.

00The story in eight steps

1 · The gap

Video models miss social structure

People watched 3-second clips of two-person interactions and judged which were alike. Across 14 pretrained vision encoders, none match that similarity structure as well as a sentence embedding of a one-line caption (MPNet) — even though participants never saw any text.

2 · The human signal

49,484 odd-one-out choices

On each trial, 245 participants picked the odd-one-out of three videos, implicitly marking the other two as more similar. A greedy set-cover design guarantees every video pair appears in at least one triplet.

3 · Local geometry

Triplet loss: pull and push

At every step, the anchor clip (A) is pulled toward the clip humans judged similar (P) and pushed away from the odd-one-out (N). Only low-rank adapters (LoRA) and a small projector are updated.

4 · Global geometry

RSA loss: match the whole map

Six times per epoch, a differentiable RSA loss aligns the model's full pairwise-distance matrix on a 24-video subset with the human similarity matrix — shaping global structure that local constraints miss.

5 · Better together

The hybrid wins

On V-JEPA 2.1, the hybrid beats each loss alone and a budget-matched triplet model — so RSA adds different information, not just more constraints. A control that distills MPNet's caption knowledge instead falls far short.

6 · The result

Nearly triple the alignment

V-JEPA 2.1 ViT-B goes from R² = 0.058 to 0.165 — 78% of the noise ceiling, past MPNet. Every backbone improves, and fine-tuned models capture variance that caption embeddings can't.

7 · Generalization

It transfers to moving shapes

On PHASE — interactions between animated triangles with no faces or bodies — the frozen BGS encoder reaches 81%, up from 55%, matching human agreement within sampling error. Action recognition is preserved.

8 · Mechanism

Attention moves to what matters

The pretrained model fixates on one region. After BGS, attention spreads across both agents, faces, gaze, and interacting hands — with no spatial supervision. Explore it below ↓

    03Pick a backbone

    BGS works across four families of pretraining — self-supervised prediction, supervised classification, masked reconstruction, and contrastive vision-language. Choose one to see how it changes:

    Not just caption knowledge

    Variance partitioning against MPNet shows two shifts after fine-tuning: structure that only the language model captured collapses into the shared component, and video-unique variance doubles (R² 0.02 → 0.04) — social-visual structure that even an idealized caption embedding cannot reach. Bootstrapping the 637 observed test pairs, V-JEPA 2.1 ViT-B rises from 0.043 [0.016, 0.080] to 0.160 [0.109, 0.216].

    05What BGS learns generalizes

    We froze the BGS-aligned V-JEPA 2.1 ViT-B encoder and tested it on PHASE: two-agent interactions rendered as moving shapes, with no faces, bodies, or scene context. A linear probe on 400 videos, evaluated on the 100-video generalization set, classifies friendly / neutral / adversarial interactions:

    3-way social interaction accuracy on PHASE
    Bars start at chance (33%)

    To our knowledge, this is the first demonstration that pixel-level video representations reach prior baselines on this benchmark — matching a hand-designed inverse-planning model and human agreement within sampling error.

    Ridge-regression probes on five densely annotated attributes show that BGS makes an interpretable social-affective geometry linearly accessible — although no attribute labels were ever used in training.

    Arousal, dominance, and valence improve substantially over the pretrained baseline.
    Intimacy improves modestly; communication declines slightly.
    The language-distilled control fails to reproduce the gains on 4 of 5 attributes — the exception is communication, where caption words like “talking” carry the answer.

    Linear probes on UCF101 (split 1, frozen backbones, 3 seeds) show no catastrophic forgetting:

    07Takeaways

    Social perception is latent in video models. Pretrained backbones carry enough information about the social world; a compact behavioral signal makes it usable.
    Relational judgments are an efficient supervision signal. Unlike preference learning they shape representations, not outputs; unlike attribute labels they capture the integrated structure of human judgment.
    Gains are general. Consistent across four backbones and pretraining objectives, with zero-shot transfer and no loss of action recognition.
