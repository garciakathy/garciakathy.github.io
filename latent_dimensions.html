The Latent Dimensions Supporting Dynamic Social Scene Perception

Kathy Garcia Leyla Isik
Johns Hopkins University
Preprint (coming soon) CCN 2026 abstract BibTeX

    All projects

    00Overview

    People recognize a huge variety of social scenes at a glance. We asked what structure organizes that knowledge — and found a compact set of interpretable, data-driven dimensions that explains human judgments and neural responses better than features derived from established social-perception theories.

    54,000
    odd-one-out triplet similarity judgments
    3 s
    naturalistic social interaction videos
    fMRI
    plus behavioral annotations for every video

    Humans rapidly recognize and understand a rich variety of social scenes, but how this information is organized in the human mind and brain is still largely unknown. We collected 54,000 odd-one-out similarity judgments on an existing dataset of short social-interaction videos that already has behavioral annotations and fMRI data, and trained a data-driven embedding model to recover the latent dimensions structuring human social-scene perception.

    01Approach

    VideosShort clips of everyday social interactions with existing annotations and fMRI.
    Judgments54k triplet odd-one-out choices: which clip doesn't belong?
    EmbeddingLearn a low-dimensional embedding that predicts every choice.
    TestCompare against theory-based ratings on held-out behavior and fMRI.

    02What the dimensions capture

    A compact set of relatively distinct dimensions captured most of the explainable variance. Rather than abstract axes, they reflect interpretable social contexts and activities. Click a dimension:

    The embedding is finer-grained than category labels: it separates contexts that a single broad category (e.g., “infants”) would lump together.

    03Results

    Better than theory-driven features on behavior. The learned embedding predicted held-out similarity judgments substantially better than human-annotated ratings grounded in established theories of social perception.
    Compact and interpretable. A small set of relatively distinct dimensions captured most of the explainable variance, each reflecting a recognizable social context or activity.
    They also predict the brain. The same dimensions predicted neural representational geometry in fMRI significantly better than predefined behavioral features across visual cortex.

    Together, these results give an empirically derived, low-dimensional mapping linking behavioral similarity judgments to neural representations for social-scene perception.

    Figures and full results will be added here when the preprint is released in October 2026.

    04How it fits together

    This project uses the same odd-one-out paradigm as our large-scale study of social scene judgments, which compared human similarity with predefined features and 350+ DNNs. Here, instead of testing predefined features, we let the dimensions emerge from the data. The resulting human similarity structure is also what behavioral geometric supervision uses to align video models.
